# Synthetic Computability and the Sheaf Theory of Oracles and Forcing

This repository contains my research notes developing domain theory internally to **Hyland's effective topos** using enriched category theory. It builds extensional synthetic computability, gives an intensional account, and proves adequacy results connecting the internal constructions with external computability over Kleene's $K_1$. It then develops oracle computation and forcing through sheaf-theoretic localization of effective domains.

## Outline by part

1. **The effective topos and positive computation.** Establishes the internal logic, Rosolini dominance, semidecidable predicates, overtness, and dominance-classified partiality.

2. **Enriched category theory over the subobject classifier.** Develops $\Omega$-enriched preorders, distributors, ideal completion, approximation, and continuous domains as retracts of free completions.

3. **Synthetic domain theory.** Constructs the $\Sigma$-admissible ideal monad and observation-equipped domains, compares them through effective points, and develops searchable rounded completions, higher-order structure, and partiality.

4. **Realizability characterization in the effective topos.** Characterizes the internal domains and their maps by tracked external presentations, using PERs for modest objects and exact-completion covers for arbitrary carriers.

5. **Extensional synthetic computability.** Constructs the partial-function domain from finite graphs and proves intrinsic results on observations, continuity, fixed points, iteration, halting, and finite information.

6. **Intensional structure and computational representation.** Develops fibred categories of names and implementations, realizer exposure, term syntax, numerical indices, recursion compilers, and execution witnesses, linked by exact interpretation to the extensional theory.

7. **Presentations of forcing spaces.** Constructs forcing locales from conditions and coverages, relating their frames and nuclei to Scott locales and ideal-domain presentations.

8. **Generic oracles and full forcing semantics.** Constructs the associated sheaf topoi, generic information, local names, and full Kripke–Joyal forcing semantics.

9. **Effective observations and oracle computation.** Extracts the semidecidable fragment of forcing observations and studies admissible restriction, quantification, evaluation, and named computation kernels.

10. **Change of base and comparison.** Compares imported ground objects with native constructions in forcing topoi, including partial evaluation and the finite-prefix reconstruction of the Cohen generic sequence.

11. **Oracle interfaces and localization.** Develops query-and-answer modalities, generated sheaf localizations, adaptive protocols, arithmetic oracles, and Artin gluing of raw implementations with their sheafified semantics.

12. **Regions, constraints, and forcing questions.** Treats conditions as regions of possible oracles and constructs universal and positive observations, with bushy-tree, DNC, Mathias-region, effectively closed-class, and finite-restraint examples.

13. **Histories, promises, and least reconstruction.** Studies realization fibres and least-history sections, with partition promises, Mathias forcing, Ellentuck covers, and Sacks perfect-tree forcing.

14. **Local candidates and descent of observations.** Constructs descent of oracle observations along local candidate spans, including Ramsey/partition forcing and prehomogeneous and homogeneous sequences.

15. **Naming obstructions and finite deformations.** Studies descent of name presentations, torsors, abelian recollement, and explicit finite-site obstructions to global naming and infinitesimal extension.

16. **Transport of computational presentations.** Constructs the comparison and transport maps for names, implementations, observations, and partial values under forcing localization and change of base.

17. **External points, stabilization, and assembly.** Relates requirements to continuous ideals and stabilized generic information, develops forcing predicates and fusion constructions, and assembles the domain-theoretic, realizability, and sheaf-theoretic comparisons.
